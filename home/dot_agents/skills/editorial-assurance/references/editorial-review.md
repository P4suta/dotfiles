# Editorial review criteria

Apply these criteria to the actual genre and publication stage.
Research, structure, line editing, copyediting, and proofreading have different objects.
Editors Canada's standards distinguish [structural editing](https://editors.ca/publications/professional-editorial-standards/structural-editing/), [stylistic editing](https://editors.ca/publications/professional-editorial-standards/stylistic-editing/), [copyediting](https://editors.ca/publications/professional-editorial-standards/copy-editing/), and [proofreading](https://editors.ca/publications/professional-editorial-standards/proofreading/).
Use the divisions below to locate defects without forcing separate documents or a fixed number of passes.

## Purpose and structure

Identify what the reader must learn or do and what the writer has promised.
Check whether the title, introduction, headings, examples, and conclusion fulfill that same purpose.
A heading must have content that answers its question.
An explanation must reach its result rather than announce that a topic is interesting or complicated.

Arrange prerequisites before their use and keep changes of subject explicit.
Check whether coordinated headings and passages answer the same organizing question.
Move a subordinate example under its parent claim instead of making it a peer category.
Compare alternatives using the same criteria and units within the same scope.
Expand missing reasoning and remove repeated conclusions, irrelevant history, generic preambles, and promotional filler.
Choose length from the evidence and requested depth rather than padding to a target.

For a narrative, inspect chronology, perspective, continuity, character motivation, and the intended emotional effect.
Preserve a deliberate rhythm, ambiguity, or nonstandard voice when it serves that purpose.
Don't impose the factual-exposition profile on fiction or an explicitly requested personal essay.

## Product copy and operational notes

Choose facts from the reader's task and existing knowledge, following [Google's audience guidance](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/audience).
Use plain Japanese in Japanese product copy.
[The plain Japanese guideline](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/kokugo_nihongo/kyoiku/pdf/92484001_01.pdf#page=7) explains selecting necessary information and simplifying the wording for the intended reader.
Name the action, visible result, and conditions needed to use the feature.
Include price, data use, permissions, and device requirements when they affect that decision.
Keep the language direct and factual.

Respect the purpose of the destination field.
A store description explains what the person can do and the conditions for using it.
Release notes identify changes the person encounters.
Review notes give the reviewer the setup and steps needed to test the app.
[Apple's App Review information](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/platform-version-information/#app-review-information) asks for information useful during review, including app-specific settings and test account details.

Remove engineering background, implementation terminology, promotional claims, and defensive statements that aren't needed for the task or the destination field.
A search prompt should tell the person what to enter.
An explanation of the matching algorithm belongs in a requested explanation of search behavior.
A review memo can require a paired device or an enabled setting without describing the framework that implements the feature.
Keep required disclosures and limitations that change the reader's action in the field where they apply.

Check the revision through the reader's task.
An empty screen must identify the next action, and an error must identify the failed operation and an available recovery.
A review memo must let the reviewer prepare and test the relevant features.
A support instruction must identify the information needed to resolve the request.
A requested mechanism explanation must keep the necessary technical details, evidence, and tradeoffs.
Remove a sentence when its removal loses no needed action, decision, condition, explanation, or required disclosure.
Keep that semantic review separate from mechanical prose checks.

## Argument structure and transitions

For factual exposition, make each paragraph a unit of reasoning with an identifiable point, following [Google's paragraph guidance](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/paragraphs).
Review the function of each passage relative to another passage, using the distinction between supporting and coordinated material in [Rhetorical Structure Theory](https://www.sfu.ca/rst/01intro/intro.html).
Use the following compact house categories to inspect relations, rather than prescribing a vocabulary of conjunctions.

For grounds and inference, name the supported claim, its evidence or premises, and the bridge to the conclusion while preserving scope and uncertainty.
For cause and result, identify the mechanism, its conditions, and its result, and establish the causal link independently of chronology.
For a condition or exception, state when the claim applies and how the exception changes the result.
For comparison or contrast, name both subjects, the shared criterion, and the relevant difference or concession.
For elaboration, attach a definition, example, detail, or restatement to the claim it explains while preserving the scope of an example.
For a sequence, supply the actual prerequisite, execution dependency, or chronology that determines the order.
For coordination, group distinct answers to a common organizing question at the same level, with any dependency stated explicitly.

Trace each material conclusion back to stated grounds and each supporting passage to the point it serves.
For a long explanation, keep a compact relation map with the working evidence and compare it with the finished text.
The map must identify the actual claims and connections, rather than record relation labels without their content.
If a passage has no relevant role, move it, combine it with its parent, or remove it.
Repair missing premises and intermediate operations before choosing a transition.

Use an explicit connective when the intended relation would otherwise be ambiguous, especially at changes of direction or inference.
Name the connected objects and the condition or mechanism inside the sentence when a bare connective leaves the link unexplained.
Don't require a connective at every sentence or paragraph, rotate synonyms for decoration, or impose frequency quotas.
Repeated additive transitions require checking whether the material answers a common question or contains unrelated points.
Read the headings and paragraph points in sequence to check that the document's question and answer remain recoverable.
Review both sentence-level cohesion and paragraph relations, following [Editors Canada's stylistic standards C1 and C2](https://editors.ca/publications/professional-editorial-standards/stylistic-editing/).
Read adjacent sentences together and identify what established object, result, or condition each sentence continues and what new contribution it makes.
Reorder information or name the continuing subject when the reader would otherwise have to reconstruct an unstated connection.
Use subordinate clauses for subordinate ideas and parallel structures for coordinated ideas, while keeping the main action recoverable.
Group independent reference material under a relevant heading when it doesn't advance an explanation.
Vary sentence construction to serve the content, as the [Australian Government Style Manual](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style/sentences#vary-sentence-structure) recommends, rather than reducing every statement to an isolated short sentence.
Apply source-line sentence boundaries as formatting while preserving connected paragraphs and reasoning.
After revision, reread complete paragraphs and repair new discontinuities introduced by shortening, splitting, or moving sentences.
Use checked-arguments for inferential validity.
A discourse relation or connective doesn't turn an empirical or causal claim into a truth-functional implication.

## Evidence and factual accuracy

Identify the exact supporting passage or reproducible observation for each consequential factual claim.
Check names, organizations, quotations, dates, locations, versions, quantities, units, and attribution.
Retrieve the primary record when the claim concerns a specification, release, legal text, experiment, or historical decision.
Verify changing information for the article's stated date.
An unsuccessful search doesn't establish that something never happened.

Read the source around the quoted passage.
Preserve its subject, scope, conditions, negation, uncertainty, and time reference.
Don't convert a proposal into an implemented feature, a benchmark into a general performance claim, or one person's statement into a consensus.
Distinguish the source's writer, publisher, reviewer, decision authority, and implementation owner when attributing a claim.
Verify the project's actual decision process before naming a body or treating a writer's explanation as an institutional position.
Make causal and motivational claims only when the cited evidence supports that relation.
Use checked-arguments for the distinction between evidence, inference, and decision relevance.

A citation must identify the source that actually supports the nearby clause.
Split a sentence when its clauses need different evidence.
State the information in the article before linking its source.
When the wording itself matters, give a short exact quotation with attribution and explain its consequence.
Compare quotations with the original rather than quoting a search snippet or another generated summary.
Identify translations and modifications to quoted material.
Avoid excessive quotation and copied structure, and check applicable reuse conditions for text, code, images, and data.
Keep private information, credentials, and invented observations out of the deliverable.

Follow the [Google developer documentation linking guidance](https://developers.google.com/style/cross-references#write-descriptive-link-text) for short, distinctive descriptions of the destination.
Match link wording to the destination's record type and subject.
Link an externally defined identifier to its issuing authority's canonical record at the meaningful mention.
Check the identifier, record contents, applicable version, and claimed relationship rather than constructing a plausible URL.

At the first substantive mention of a public participant whose identity matters to an attributed claim, give a verified full name, relevant role, and profile link.
Prefer an institutional biography, a personal website, or a project account whose identity a primary record establishes.
Use surnames later only when the identity remains unambiguous, and avoid repeating the profile link at every mention.
A profile identifies the participant.
Keep the separate citation that supports the attributed statement or decision.
Verify the destination and identity rather than inferring an account from a plausible name or URL.
This house choice applies the [Google linking guidance](https://developers.google.com/style/cross-references#choose-links-selectively) and [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) link-purpose criterion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/link-purpose-in-context.html) to participant attribution.

## Explanatory substance

Complete the causal or operational explanation behind every claim of importance, clarity, difficulty, or design change.
Name the actor, input, action, condition, and result where they determine the explanation.
Give the specific case and walk through the intermediate steps.
Explain each named alternative's mechanism and the documented reason for its rejection or limitation.
A resource or complexity claim must identify the affected operation and quantity, or describe the concrete implementation burden when no measurement exists.

Delete a writer's reaction when the factual article didn't request it.
Delete routine research declarations and reminders of obvious distinctions.
Replace a vague benefit with the behavior that differs and the case in which it differs.
Do the verification promised by a sentence about compatibility or operational impact.
Don't end by assigning unanswered research questions to the reader.
Preserve a real procedure's necessary instructions and a source's relevant technical limitation.

## Sentences, paragraphs, and consistency

Check the subject-predicate relationship, pronoun antecedents, modifier attachment, logical connectives, tense, register, and semantic roles.
Resolve a sentence that permits materially different interpretations.
Use concrete verbs and named objects in place of unexplained abstractions.
Choose the subject that expresses the mechanism or responsibility relevant to the claim.
Attribute deliberation and judgment to identified participants rather than disguising the actor behind a document or quotation.
An inanimate subject can still describe an actual operation or a document's normative content.
Keep one paragraph's sentences connected to its main point.
Split independent points while preserving their logical relationship.
Remove duplicated information even when it uses different words.

Check grammar, spelling, usage, punctuation, agreement, capitalization, abbreviations, terminology, and notation.
Use a compact style sheet when the text or collection contains recurring choices.
Keep verified names, identifiers, and literal quotations intact.
Check cross-references, labels, captions, alternative text, metadata, references, and quantitative totals along with the body.
Distinguish percentages from percentage points and supply the denominator, baseline, period, and units required to interpret a number.
Recalculate derived values rather than accepting a plausible-looking figure.

For Japanese, keep the chosen register, punctuation, character forms, loanword spelling, and units consistent with the house style.
The Japan Translation Federation's [Japanese style guide](https://www.jtf.jp/index.php/tips/styleguide) supplies an orthography and notation reference for translation work.
Use a lexical source for an uncertain reading, and preserve semantic spaces inside Latin expressions and numeric units.
For other languages, use their native dictionaries and rules rather than importing an English rule without checking its linguistic basis.

## Translation

Compare the translation with the source at the clause and document levels.
Check omissions, additions, reversal of conditions, modal strength, negation, quantities, technical terms, and references.
Resolve each technical expression's referent, operations, state, and constraints before choosing its spelling.
An English form or loanword doesn't explain a specialized concept by itself.
Preserve distinctions such as required, recommended, allowed, and experimental.
Make the target text natural without inventing motives, experiences, or facts.
Identify a translated quotation and keep the original passage in the working evidence when its wording bears on the argument.

## Proof and delivery

Inspect the intended rendered medium, including headings, navigation, lists, quotes, tables, code, mathematics, images, captions, and footnotes.
Regenerate the requested output from the revised source and verify its content after promotion from any temporary build directory.
Check clipping, overflow, page breaks, orphaned labels, character corruption, and missing assets.
Open links and confirm that their destinations are the intended records.
Make image descriptions and reading order usable without relying on color or visual position alone.
The [Australian Government Style Manual](https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/editing-and-proofreading) covers ancillary content that meets accessibility needs and a final proofreading stage.

Check that the title, date, byline, excerpt, description, language, and publication state match the final text.
Repeat affected checks after changes and compare the final rendered artifact with the approved content.
Keep research notes and check logs outside the reader-facing prose unless the requested format needs them.
Don't claim publication, source verification, native-language validation, or executable-example validation unless that operation actually succeeded.
